\title[Qualifying Four-Camera Stereo on One Edge NPU]{Qualifying Four-Camera Stereo on One Edge NPU: Operating Points, Tested Scaling Limits, and a Value-Preserving Repair of Block-Floating-Point Loss}
\begin{abstract}
A quadruped robot using four 30-Hz stereo cameras for foothold perception requires 120 stereo-pair inferences per second from a single embedded accelerator. We evaluate whether an AMD XDNA2 neural processing unit (NPU) can sustain this workload using artifact-bound protocols that distinguish disparity accuracy, NPU-only placement, output validation, and sustained runtime.
% EXP-EDIT-BEGIN: ABSTRACT_RESULTS | E01,E02,E07,E12
With a common model and synchronized replay, a process-isolated two-process organization sustained 120.0 pair-FPS with 24.31-ms p99 response, while a single-session joint organization sustained 119.97 pair-FPS with 28.44-ms p99 response; each passed its frozen replay-runtime criteria in three 600-s trials. Across two development-exposed 20-scene KITTI subsets, the same model achieved lower endpoint error than the device-default stereo method in every scene and seed ($p_{\mathrm{holm}}=3.81\times10^{-6}$), with no corresponding bad3 advantage claimed.
Nominal single-pair latency did not determine the sustained multi-stream outcome. An 8.08-ms speed-line model failed every sustained four-stream check with one shared session but passed all twelve stream-window checks with four process-isolated sessions at the same offered load. For that model, joint serving reduced invocation time relative to four separate calls yet still replaced frames near 30 FPS per stream.
Separately, a value-preserving channel permutation recovered 98.0\% of a separate model's synthetic-development Bad-1 increase after deployment, without retraining; that model still exceeded the serialized four-stream service budget. Physical tests with four stereo cameras met the registered 30.0-FPS-per-stream criterion for both speed-line artifacts after one-decimal rounding, each over three 600-s trials with no replacements, but no completed OAK-camera output arrived within one 33.333-ms frame interval after exposure end. These results separate throughput qualification from numerical fidelity and camera-to-output freshness on the tested installation.
% EXP-EDIT-END: ABSTRACT_RESULTS
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The target quadruped configuration uses four stereo cameras beneath its body for foothold perception (Fig.~\ref{fig:QuadCam}). The deployment objective is to produce all four disparity streams on a single edge accelerator.

\begin{figure}[b]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.7\linewidth]{fig/quad_cam.png}
    \caption{Target quadruped camera arrangement and view roles. This configuration motivates the workload; the heterogeneous physical-camera measurements are described separately.}
    \Description{A schematic showing the target quadruped configuration with four stereo-camera locations and their view roles. The schematic motivates the workload and is not evidence of synchronized four-camera operation.}
    \label{fig:QuadCam}
\end{figure}

Classical stereo uses explicit matching costs and optimization rules, whereas learning-based methods trade accuracy against computational cost. Their embedded deployment must nevertheless be evaluated under the intended multi-camera workload (Section~\ref{sec:related}).

We ask whether one NPU can sustain 120 stereo-pair inferences per second for four approximately 30-FPS streams, meeting the specified response criteria without host neural execution.

The \emph{runtime-control} track holds weights fixed. It compares sequential, process-isolated logical, and joint-input organizations; \emph{specialization} trains a model per organization. Accuracy and runtime remain artifact-specific, and process isolation does not imply physical partitioning. A second campaign on the same accelerator characterizes block floating point with another model and criterion. Its speed-first line admits runtime by rate, replacement, and integrity, without an accuracy floor; accelerator accuracy is reported separately. Specialization retains its development-accuracy gate.

This work contributes the following.
% EXP-EDIT-BEGIN: CONTRIBUTION_RESULTS | E01,E02,E03,E07,E12
\begin{itemize}
\item A common-model four-stream operating point: process-isolated pair serving and synchronized joint replay pass the frozen runtime gates in three 600-s repetitions, while timing, integrity, and incomplete outcomes remain distinct.
\item A two-artifact, three-organization comparison at fixed offered load: one artifact fails shared-session checks but passes with four process-isolated sessions; physical-camera tests expose additional capture delay.
\item A negative result for a frozen quantization-aware training/export procedure: all eight full-precision candidates pass development accuracy admission, none of their fixed 8-bit exports do, and an accuracy-admitted recovery fails its first board-output check.
\item A value-preserving permutation that repairs block floating point loss on silicon, with emulated random-permutation controls, an unrepaired device-default shortfall, and two geometric repairs that fail out of sample.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
Neural stereo estimation spans recurrent, foundation-model, and efficiency-oriented architectures: RAFT-Stereo is recurrent~\cite{raftstereo}, FoundationStereo~\cite{foundationstereo} and its real-time variant~\cite{fastfoundationstereo} target zero-shot generalization, and MobileStereoNet reduces parameters and operations with mobile blocks~\cite{shamsafar2022mobilestereonet}; on edge hardware the matcher has been compressed by distillation then pruning~\cite{rbq_pan2024distillprune} and by residual and attention blocks~\cite{rbq_liang2026rcaenet}. The graphs here belong to the reduced-resolution cost-volume family with two-dimensional aggregation, as in HITNet's tile hypotheses~\cite{rbq_tankovich2021hitnet} and LightStereo's channel-boosted three-dimensional volume~\cite{rbq_guo2025lightstereo}. The question here is complementary to architectural accuracy and efficiency: whether a fixed exported artifact passes multi-stream placement, timing, and output-validation checks. This study claims no new stereo architecture.

Multi-camera edge systems exploit content overlap: CrossRoI removes redundant regions across overlapping views~\cite{guo2021crossroi} and MOSAIC packs selected regions from concurrent streams into one inference canvas~\cite{gokarn2023mosaic}; our joint arrangement amortizes an invocation but preserves complete, independent stereo pairs and makes no content-selection claim.

For inference systems, predictable execution~\cite{clockwork}, fine-grained GPU sharing~\cite{salus}, batched video-DNN scheduling~\cite{nexus}, preemptive time-multiplexing~\cite{prema}, colocation on a fissionable array~\cite{planaria} and, on device, multi-DNN coordination~\cite{rbq_jeong2022band} and preemption on mobile edge GPUs~\cite{rbq_han2024pantheon} motivate separating service time, queueing, batching and tail response. These mechanisms are distinct from the ordinary processes and sessions used here, where process count and session ownership are the exposed deployment controls.

Reliability studies trace injected faults through accelerators~\cite{li2017dnnresilience}, protect inference from bit flips~\cite{zheng2025save} or restrict ranges~\cite{rbq_chen2021ranger}, and field reports describe processors computing incorrect results without raising any error, visible only against a reference~\cite{rbq_hochschild2021cores}. They motivate the end-to-end output validation used here as an admission condition rather than a statistic.

Narrow numerical formats for inference increasingly take the form of block floating point, a group of adjacent elements sharing one exponent, each keeping a sign and a short mantissa. Microsoft Floating Point introduced it at production scale~\cite{rbq_rouhani2020msfp}; it was standardized as the Microscaling formats~\cite{rbq_rouhani2023microscaling} and by the Open Compute Project~\cite{rbq_ocp2023mx}, and architectural studies examine such blocks for training~\cite{rbq_zhang2022fast} and serving~\cite{rbq_lee2025mxplus}. The accelerator here exposes a format of the same family~\cite{rbq_amdquarkbfp16}, and because its block boundaries fall along a convolution's input-channel axis, the assignment of channels to blocks is a property of the graph rather than of the format: this work treats that assignment as the quantity to be chosen.

A second thread makes quantization groups more homogeneous by reordering the tensor rather than rewriting it: clustering activation channels by observed range~\cite{rbq_yuan2023rptq}, sorting by a joint statistic of activations and weights~\cite{rbq_cheng2026permuquant}, pairing an outlier channel with low-magnitude companions~\cite{rbq_yao2026ocgquant}, and, outside quantization, permuting a weight matrix so that different weights survive an N:M pruning pattern~\cite{rbq_pool2021permutations}. These differ from transforms that repair quantization by changing values---cross-layer equalization~\cite{rbq_nagel2019dfq}, range migration into the weights~\cite{rbq_xiao2023smoothquant}, activation-aware scaling~\cite{rbq_lin2024awq}, Hadamard rotation~\cite{rbq_ashkboos2024quarot}---and from post-calibration parameter repairs, adaptive rounding~\cite{rbq_nagel2020adaround} and bias correction~\cite{rbq_nagel2019dfq}. The present rewrite belongs to the reordering approach: it preserves stored parameter values while changing their assignment to quantization blocks. The network function is unchanged in exact arithmetic; that identity does not imply bitwise equality after finite-precision deployment. Ground-truth-free selection is not, by itself, the novelty claim; the permutation here was selected on thirty-two fixed training frames.

Studies of deployment on commercial accelerators repeatedly find that vendor abstractions do not predict real graphs, whether through the software stack~\cite{rbq_hadidi2019characterizing}, operator fusion the operator list hides~\cite{rbq_zhang2021nnmeter}, the compiler's mapping to the memory hierarchy~\cite{rbq_seshadri2022edgetpu} or undisclosed microarchitecture~\cite{rbq_park2026lens}; the observations here are of that kind.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:prob}
Pair throughput counts disparity maps per second; invocation throughput counts accelerator calls. Four 30-FPS cameras require 120 pair-FPS. Serialized single-session serving allows 8.333 ms per one-pair call, whereas four-pair joint serving requires 30 calls per second and allows 33.333 ms per invocation. These mean service budgets exclude queueing and host work; they are not per-session limits for concurrent organizations.

% EXP-EDIT-BEGIN: PROTOCOL_RESULTS | E01,E02,E04,E12
Live-input response is measured from capture to completion of disparity decoding. In camera-free replay, the pair harness measures from each frame's scheduled arrival, including dispatch lag, and the joint harness from bundle release. The reported joint replay uses synchronized arrivals with zero bundle-assembly wait. Both exclude capture, transport and camera preprocessing, and their p99 pools completed stream outputs in a window. For $n$ streams the replay gate requires exactly $n$ streams, at least 29.5 FPS each and $29.5n$ aggregate pair-FPS, a minimum/maximum rate ratio of at least 0.98, replacement at most 2\%, p99 at most 40 ms, no raw mismatch and no CPU neural node. The frozen six-stream form of the aggregate criterion was 177 pair-FPS; the same rule at four streams gives 118 pair-FPS, a proportional restatement and not a new measurement. These finite-window criteria are distinct from the registered rounded-rate target: four streams and three 600-s repetitions with every stream reaching 30.0 FPS after rounding half up to one decimal.

With uniformly staggered 30-FPS arrivals, the earliest frame in a same-cycle four-pair batch waits 25.0 ms, leaving 15.0 ms of a 40-ms per-frame budget for post-assembly work. This bound neither applies to mixed-cycle/partial batches nor predicts pooled p99 without the post-release distribution.

The two campaigns are judged under non-nested replacement rules: this replay gate allows 2\% replacement and requires p99 at most 40 ms, while the second campaign's preregistrations admit no replaced frame and state no response criterion. On the reported rate, replacement, and response criteria, S5's joint windows would satisfy the replay limits whereas its sequential windows would not; S11's joint windows fail under either rule. This comparison is not a new full-protocol qualification under the other campaign's gate.
% EXP-EDIT-END: PROTOCOL_RESULTS

Non-passing outcomes are \emph{performance-negative} (specified output checks pass but timing, rate, or replacement fails), \emph{integrity-negative} (bound-oracle raw mismatch), \emph{incomplete} (required measurement missing), or \emph{unknown} (conditional gate unevaluated). A raw mismatch excludes a configuration regardless of throughput. Missing evidence never becomes a pass, and CPU neural inference is never substituted.

\section{System and Model}

\subsection{Network structure and input--output definitions}
% EXP-EDIT-BEGIN: MODEL_IO | E02,E05,E09
The common runtime-control network, denoted HWGT128, receives rectified grayscale images from the left and right cameras, each of dimensions $1\times1\times256\times416$. Convolutional features form a disparity-indexed matching volume, followed by aggregation, a learned coupled moment projector, and phase-residual refinement. It uses a width multiplier of 1.5 and a two-layer moment projector with 256 channels, and its output has seven channels at $128\times208$: coarse disparity terms, one auxiliary channel and four phase residuals.

Its inputs use UINT8 with a scale of $1/32$ and a zero point of 114; the output scale and zero point are $1/32$ and 42, and the seven channel multipliers are $(4,4,16,4,4,4,4)$. We use \emph{XINT8} for a fixed static deployment artifact with UINT8 activations and signed 8-bit weights, scored with the frozen decoder; its admission rule requires every raw replay output to match the frozen CPU oracle bit for bit. The rule and the implemented comparison coverage are distinct: sampled probes alone cannot establish full-output equality. Decoding adds and upsamples the first two channels and rearranges the last four into a $2\times2$ residual pattern added to the result, separating cameras before resizing; it is timed and runs no neural layer on the CPU. The specialization track keeps the same input tier, disparity units, output shape and decoder but trains a separate model per execution family: S6 uses hidden widths of 128, 96 and 64 channels, L6 256, 192 and 128, and J6 128 and 96. Each of the eight seed-0 candidates follows a 32-epoch full-precision trajectory and, after the exposed development gate, a fixed 64-epoch quantization-aware one whose terminal epochs alone are eligible. For HWGT128 hardware execution, odd scalar activation zero points were advanced to the next even value, the maximum unsigned value handled separately, and an identity depthwise requantization inserted before a nearest-neighbour resize; that adjustment changes the model and was separately evaluated.
% EXP-EDIT-END: MODEL_IO

\subsection{Runtime operation and validation}
% EXP-EDIT-BEGIN: VALIDATION_SCOPE | E02,E04
Each session disables host-provider and Python fallback and inspects its compiled context; provider selection alone does not ensure full accelerator placement~\cite{ortapi,amddeploy}. Offline host execution supplies references only. Each stream has an 8-frame FIFO; overflow discards the oldest frame and increments replacements, including overwritten publications in the physical reader. Queue wait ends at dequeue and begins at scheduled arrival in replay (including dispatch lag) or ring publication in physical tests. Failures archive inputs, raw outputs, input--output definitions, and model hashes; a later matching replay does not erase them. Full-tensor references, repeat checks, timed 64-element probes, and post-window replays remain distinct; sampled equality does not establish equality of untested elements.
% EXP-EDIT-END: VALIDATION_SCOPE

\section{Execution Organizations}
\label{sec:fam}
% EXP-EDIT-BEGIN: ORG_T1 | E04
A verdict is specific to its artifact, organization, workload, and protocol. \textbf{T1} uses one full-array session, serving one pair per call without an all-camera barrier. It selects the queue with the earliest capture-stamped head pair, rotating ties, then dequeues FIFO; submission and decoding also consume the 8.333-ms budget.
% EXP-EDIT-END: ORG_T1

% EXP-EDIT-BEGIN: ORG_T2L | E04,E09
In \textbf{T2L}, each process owns one session; $k\times m$ means $k$ processes serving $m$ cameras each. Common-model four-stream tests use $2\times2$ versus T1 $1\times4$; speed-line tests use $4\times1$. Calls remain one-pair, with no assigned hardware columns. Workers own buffers and contexts, allow one outstanding call, and share the arrival schedule/clock to preserve phase, jitter, and scene identity. Each loads a private embedded provider context from a verified cache~\cite{amdcontext}.
% EXP-EDIT-END: ORG_T2L

% EXP-EDIT-BEGIN: ORG_T3 | E09
\textbf{T3} processes all independent camera pairs per call through multiple inputs/outputs (two inputs, one output, and one DPU branch per camera in one session), channel packing at batch one, or canvas tiling followed by independent networks. Static batch-four rewrites were rejected and are treated separately (Section~\ref{sec:speedline}). Camera-isolation checks perturb one camera and compare outputs against single-camera references. No organization constructs a panorama or removes a stereo computation.
% EXP-EDIT-END: ORG_T3

% EXP-EDIT-BEGIN: ORG_RESIDENCY | E02,E09
Historical T2 identifiers denote proposed physical region assignment, not T2L measurements. Two two-session residency censuses placed both process identifiers in one context occupying all eight columns. Concurrent execution also produced a raw mismatch; neither physical partitioning nor an accepted speedup was demonstrated.
% EXP-EDIT-END: ORG_RESIDENCY

\section{Evaluation}
Four model groups retain separate criteria: HWGT128 uses scene means against the device default and the replay-runtime gate; specialized S6/L6/J6 candidates use macro-medians against that default and never reach the board. The second campaign's R6/S5/S11 use frozen classical profiles, with device-default accuracy and runtime verdicts reported separately.

\subsection{Scope and comparator}
\label{sec:scope}
% EXP-EDIT-BEGIN: EVAL_SCOPE | E05,E10,E12
The board contains an AMD Ryzen AI 7 350 processor and its XDNA2 NPU. The host kernel driver was build 2.21.260102.53 throughout and neither it nor the firmware was replaced. The runtime-control campaign ran on XRT 2.21.75 with the vendor SDK 1.7.1 runtime; the campaign of Section~\ref{sec:hw} ran a vendor SDK 1.8 userspace with ONNX Runtime 1.27.0 and XRT 2.25.37 over that same driver, a pairing its own run records mark as unverified and not an official matched stack.

Historical live tests replicated one camera pair across logical inputs, including capture through decoding; terminal controls used camera-free replay. Neither establishes independent four-camera operation, synchronization, or distance accuracy. Section~\ref{sec:physical} reports heterogeneous speed-line physical tests without accuracy or synchronization measurements.

Accuracy uses twenty development-exposed scenes each from KITTI 2015 and KITTI 2012. EPE is mean absolute disparity error in source-image pixels; bad3 is the percentage exceeding three pixels, not KITTI D1~\cite{kittistereo}. Both methods use the same ground-truth-valid pixels, retaining numeric firmware disparities at device-invalid locations. Predictions are bilinearly resampled to the source grid; disparity-value scaling is separate. A nearest-neighbor validity mask is used only for density/hole diagnostics.

The device-hardware comparator uses \textsc{default} stereo settings: $1280\times800$ input, factor-two decimation to $640\times400$ output, and disparity in input-pixel units~\cite{luxonisstereodepth}. No weighted median filter or tuned postprocessing was added. Rectification was disabled for already-rectified benchmark images. Eight static-pair repeats settled temporal state before scoring; moving scenes were not evaluated. This is the sole first-campaign admission comparator.

A host classical baseline reaches 1.4407 px macro-median EPE and 9.39\% bad3 at 36.0 ms per pair, but differing tier/coverage rules make it contextual only. It is also selection-exposed: the best of 288 settings on the same twenty scenes.

\subsection{Common-model accuracy and uncertainty}
\label{sec:acc}

% EXP-EDIT-BEGIN: COMMON_ACCURACY | E02,E05,E10,E12
All three hardware-adapted HWGT128 seed graphs matched their unoptimized CPU references exactly on the forty development inputs, so the figures below are accelerator outputs, not training graphs. Averaging the three quantization-aware seeds, which share one pretrained parent and so characterize variability from the quantization-aware stage rather than independent full training runs, within each development scene and then across scenes, without combining predictions, gives 1.826 pixels EPE and 13.063\% bad3 on KITTI 2015 against the device default's 4.555 and 13.251\%, and 1.898 and 13.052\% on KITTI 2012 against 6.095 and 15.417\%; mean and median were lower for every seed than for the comparator.

Timing uses archived seed 0, not the seed average: its EPE agrees to three decimals, with bad3 of 13.058\% and 13.040\%.

Bad3 improvement is inconsistent across scenes. KITTI 2015 has mean paired difference $-0.187$ points, exploratory 95\% scene-bootstrap interval $[-1.228,0.862]$, ten improving and ten worsening scenes (exact two-sided sign-test $p=1.0$), and a higher scene-level 90th percentile: 26.77\% versus 23.90\%. These results establish no consistent or statistically confirmed bad3 advantage.

Applying the same exact two-sided paired sign test and Holm correction used in Section~\ref{sec:e2} separates the two metrics: endpoint error improves in twenty of twenty scenes on both datasets, for the seed average and for each seed, at $p_{\mathrm{holm}} = 3.81\times10^{-6}$, whereas bad3 improves in ten of twenty scenes on KITTI 2015 ($p = 1.0$) and thirteen of twenty on KITTI 2012 ($p_{\mathrm{holm}} = 0.526$).

Uncertainty uses 20,000 paired scene resamples, not independent pixels or sixty seed--scene observations. Unverified drive independence and teacher-cache/checkpoint association make the intervals exploratory, not final-accuracy evidence.

\subsection{Execution-specialized offboard qualification}

\subsubsection{Channel permutation recovers block floating-point loss}
% EXP-EDIT-BEGIN: BFP_RESULTS | E02,E07,E09,E10
That path carries BF16 tensors at the model boundary, but its convolutions are block floating point~\cite{rbq_amdquarkbfp16}: consecutive input channels form blocks of eight sharing one exponent, so one large coefficient reduces the precision available beside it. Permuting a producing convolution's output channels together with the matching input columns of every consumer leaves each summed product unchanged, so the rewrite is an identity in exact arithmetic while block composition changes completely. One permutation over nine producer--consumer pairs was selected by a preregistered ground-truth-free search on 32 fixed training frames, its budget and selection rule fixed before any result existed. Three separate structural checks, including one on the board before any hardware call, agreed that parent and rewrite share node count, operator histogram, node sequence, boundary and parameter count, 198 of 214 initializers being byte identical and the other 16 rearrangements with unchanged sorted value multisets.

On the synthetic population, the deployed candidate's Bad-1 increased by 9.025 percentage points, from 41.329\% to 50.354\%, on the accelerator. A $2\times2$ factorial over \{parent, permuted\} and two optimization levels, each cell one isolated, audited session of 1,028 calls with every repeat byte exact and all operators on the accelerator, measures Bad-1---pixels whose disparity error exceeds one native pixel---on that campaign's 512-pair development population. On the synthetic and driving populations the full-precision source gives 41.329 and 39.525\%, the parent 50.354 and 38.651 at level 1, and the permuted graph 41.511 and 38.075, against a level-1 prediction of 41.514 and 38.038; the frozen classical baselines are 41.683 and 46.866 for HH and 42.585 and 41.826 for the three-path profile.

The rewrite recovered 8.844 of 9.025 Bad-1 points (98.0\%), leaving 0.182 relative to full precision. Recovery uses unrounded measurements: $50.35432-41.51051=8.84381$. The unchanged offboard block-floating-point hypothesis differed from silicon by 0.003 points synthetic/0.037 driving. The parent fails the classical-profile gate; the permutation passes all four comparisons at levels 1 and 2, with density 1.0 versus 0.73--0.79.

Seventeen random realizable permutations span 43.080--54.428 on the same \emph{predicted} metric; both preregistered selections lie below them (descriptive rank $1/18 \approx 0.056$). Selection on disjoint training frames differed by 0.377 points.

The aggregate interaction between block errors remains unexplained. Repairing the most conspicuous local block pathology exactly---a coarse-disparity channel of magnitude up to 131 sharing an eight-block with four context channels of magnitude 2--4, whose relative errors of 0.38--0.49 are the only ones above 0.11---lowers those to 0.005--0.02 but makes the predicted score \emph{worse}, from 50.328 to 53.154: block errors interact rather than add.

Across five variants of one model line the \emph{predicted} residual between the permuted graph and its full-precision source was $+2.273$, $+0.780$, $+0.185$, $+0.508$ and $+1.083$ points; only the deployed variant has a hardware counterpart, whose measured residual is $+0.182$. The effect is variant-dependent and does not replace training.

\subsubsection{Where that miss comes from, and two repairs that failed}
\label{sec:e2diag}
% EXP-EDIT-BEGIN: GEOMETRIC_DIAGNOSTICS | E05,E10,E12
A preregistered diagnostic examined the 1.0857-percentage-point bad3 gap. The rescale path from the working tier, with a perfect tier prediction, costs 0.0444 points, or 4.1\% of the gap, so this diagnostic does not identify rescaling as the dominant source of the gap, while the best tested phase-interleave decoder variant changes the score by 0.7034 points, equivalent to 64.8\% of the gap, its best variant winning 20 of 20 scenes at $p_{\mathrm{holm}} = 3.8\times10^{-6}$.

The decoder contribution has an exact origin in code: the packer centres each cell on tier pixel $(2i, 2j)$ while the decoder places its four outputs in the canvas block whose centroid is $(2i+0.5, 2j+0.5)$, so the lattices differ by half a canvas pixel in both axes and the code-derived correction is a displacement of $-0.5$ tier pixels in each, shared by training and evaluation rather than cancelling.

Two preregistered attempts to apply it failed and \emph{nothing was changed}. The first improved every driving metric and the synthetic Bad-1 but worsened synthetic Bad-3, from 17.5666 to 17.6704 with 45 of 256 frame wins at raw $p = 7.0\times10^{-27}$ (exact two-sided sign-test value; no familywise Holm-adjusted value is asserted here), failing three clauses of its rule; its $-0.66$ native pixel disparity bias is not predicted by the geometry, which accounts for 22\% of it.

The second folded the displacement into the frozen resize so the prediction is sampled once, and worsened Bad-1, Bad-3 and EPE on all eight decision populations, every one significantly, with $p_{\mathrm{holm}}$ between $1.9\times10^{-8}$ and $1.9\times10^{-63}$, while improving them on the artifact it had been selected on; a blur-matched zero-displacement control is near-neutral elsewhere, so the harm is the displacement. These decoder tests use frames, $n = 256$ per population, with Holm correction within each metric across eight decision populations. They report effects below 0.02 px; their sample unit and population differ from the twenty-scene comparator tests, and statistical significance does not establish practical importance. \emph{The sign of the benefit flips with the artifact.} A post-hoc correction with an exact derivation in code and a significant result on the artifact that motivated it can still be artifact-specific, and only validation elsewhere exposed that; the frozen decoder stands.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
% EXP-EDIT-BEGIN: CONCLUSION_RESULTS | E01,E02,E03,E07,E12
Common-model process-isolated pair serving and synchronized joint replay each pass three 600-s repetitions, with no qualification-check mismatch or CPU neural node. Six-stream screens fail without resolving capacity above four streams; one stream passes while two are integrity-negative. On two development-exposed KITTI subsets, EPE improves in every scene ($p_{\mathrm{holm}}=3.81\times10^{-6}$); no bad3 advantage is established.

S5/S11 also pass their respective heterogeneous four-camera runtime protocols, without validating HWGT128, near-range accuracy, or cross-camera synchronization; no OAK output completes within a frame interval after exposure end. All eight specialized 8-bit exports fail accuracy admission, and the recovery fails first-output checks in all three organizations. R6 recovers 98.0\% of synthetic-development Bad-1 deployment loss without retraining but still misses one device-default comparison; two geometric repairs fail to generalize.

These results qualify throughput on one installation, not closed-loop foothold perception. Execution organization, arithmetic fidelity, and capture delay require separate evaluation; none follows from nominal invocation latency alone. Integrity claims cover only the comparisons performed.
